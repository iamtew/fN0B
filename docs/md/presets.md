# Presets

The preset bar at the top shows the current preset. The arrows on either end step through the list, and clicking the middle opens it directly.

fN0B ships forty-one. The first six keep their original indices so old sessions do not jump. Presets 31–40 are bipolar: noon is rest, left and right are opposite characters.

## Warm Drive

A soft-clip saturator followed by a gentle low-pass.

- Drive: 0 dB to +18 dB
- Cutoff: 20 kHz down to 4 kHz

Starts as a clean boost and ends warm and rounded. The filter closes as the drive comes up, so it never gets harsh. Good on guitars, synths, and vocals.

## Filter Funk

A resonant low-pass with drive riding along.

- Cutoff: 200 Hz to 8 kHz
- Resonance: 0.2 to 0.9
- Drive: 0 dB to +12 dB

A manual auto-wah. The resonance climbs as the filter opens, and a little warmth creeps in with it. Great on bass, keys, and anything that wants to talk.

## Bitcrush Destruct

Sample-rate reduction, then bit-depth reduction, then a soft clip.

- Sample rate: 1x to 16x reduction
- Bit depth: 16 to 4 bits
- Drive: 0 dB to +12 dB

Clean to digital grit to full destruction. The clip at the end keeps it from being painfully bright. Fun on drums and synths.

## Tape Echo

A delay line whose repeats pass through a saturator and a damper before feeding back, then a high-cut on the output.

- Delay: 60 to 450 ms
- Repeats: 15% to 60% feedback, saturated each pass
- Top end: 12 kHz down to 2.5 kHz

A turn stretches the repeats and each pass loses a little top and gains a little grit, the way tape chews a signal. Longer settings bend the pitch while you move the knob, which is the point. Good on vocals, synths, and anything that wants to trail off.

## Shimmer Verb

A reverb whose tail feeds a loop that replays it at double speed, so it comes back an octave up, then a tone filter.

- Room: 15% to 100%
- Wet: 10% to 45%
- Shimmer: 0% to 50% of the octave bloom
- Tone: 20 kHz down to 6 kHz

Starts as a small room and ends as a washed-out bloom with a rising octave sheen. The doubled tail is raw on purpose; that roughness is the character. Pad food.

## Phaser Sweep

A six-stage all-pass phaser with its own feedback, output trimmed so the notches stay musical.

- Sweep rate: 0.12 Hz to 3.5 Hz
- Depth: 20% to 100%
- Feedback: 10% to 70%

Slow breathing on chords, jet plane by the top of the knob. The trim keeps the comb peaks polite even with the feedback high.

## Chorus Dimension

Dual modulated delays, then a little extra width.

- Rate, depth/detune, mix and width rise together

Gentle thickening into a wide, doubling-like chorus.

## Dual Delay Cascade

A short delay into a longer one, shared feedback, high-cut on the loop.

- Both times stretch, repeats densify, the loop darkens

Tight rhythmic taps that turn into cascading trails.

## Tremolo Pulse

Amplitude tremolo that can square up and bite the peaks.

- Rate 1.2 Hz to 12 Hz, depth, sine to square, a little drive

Amp throb to a rhythmic stutter.

## Space Station

Modulated delay into a large FDN, with pitch drift on the repeats.

- Size scales delay and decay together, modulation and feedback rise

Intimate space to a slightly unstable sci-fi atmosphere.

## Crush & Bloom

Saturation, an opening high-pass, then a short plate.

- Drive to +18 dB, HP 80 to 400 Hz, reverb mix and size bloom

Tight distorted punch that explodes into a bloom. Drums, bass, aggressive synths.

## Meltglass

Saturation into a slow chorus, then a closing low-pass.

- Drive 0 to +18 dB, chorus depth 0 to 70%, rate 0.08 to 0.4 Hz, LP 20 kHz to 4 kHz

Starts clean. Ends warm, smeared, dreamy.

## Parking Garage

- Comb 8 to 45 ms, feedback 10 to 85%, wet 5 to 60%, size 20 to 100%

Subtle reflections to huge parking-garage resonance.

## Broken Broadcast

## Tape Ghost

## Rubber Room

## Laser Hive

## Velvet Damage

## Event Horizon

## Shatterbox

## Filter Fork

Resonant low-pass one way, resonant high-pass the other. Noon is both filters open.

- Left: cutoff 20 kHz to 200 Hz, resonance 0.2 to 0.7
- Right: high-pass 20 Hz to 2.5 kHz, resonance 0.2 to 0.7

DJ filter on one knob. Dark, then open, then thin.

## Tiltfork

A tone see-saw. Distinct from Hypercolor, which only goes bright and also widens and excites.

- Left: tilt −8 dB (bass up, treble down)
- Noon: flat
- Right: tilt +8 dB (treble up, bass down)

## Width Rift

Image in or image out. Noon is the record as it landed.

- Left: width 100% to 0% (mono)
- Right: width 100% to 180%

## Pitch Pendulum

Octave door. Gravity Well only dives; Singularity only climbs.

- Left: 0 to −12 st
- Noon: unison, dry
- Right: 0 to +12 st
- Mix follows the throw

## Backspin

Spin it back or throw it forward. Time Parasite is reverse-only.

- Left: reverse window 40 to 300 ms, wet 0 to 50%
- Noon: dry
- Right: forward delay 40 to 450 ms, feedback 0 to 55%, wet 0 to 50%

## Halo Rail

Halo (chorus) one way, rail (flange) the other.

- Noon: light chorus, about 12 ms, depth 12%, mix 20%
- Left: delay 12 to 28 ms, depth 15 to 70%, rate 0.15 to 0.8 Hz, width 100 to 150%
- Right: delay 12 to 2 ms, feedback 0 to 70%, depth 20 to 90%

## Hollow Jet

Same phaser, opposite comb. Phaser Sweep only adds more of one direction.

- Noon: mild sweep, about 0.3 Hz, depth 25%, feedback 0
- Left: feedback 0 to −70% (hollow notches)
- Right: feedback 0 to +70% (jet)
- Rate and depth grow with how far you throw it

## Rust Chrome

- Left: 16 to 6 bits, 1x to 8x, wow, high shelf 0 to −10 dB
- Right: high shelf 0 to +8 dB, exciter 0 to 55%

## Squeeze Bloom

- Noon: bypass
- Right: short reverb, mix 0 to 45%, size 10 to 55%

## Satin Fold

Satin warmth vs folded metal. Warm Drive is only the satin side; Bone Saw is only the fold.

- Left: soft-clip 0 to +18 dB, low-pass 20 kHz to 6 kHz
- Noon: clean
- Right: wavefold 0 to 80%, hard clip on the way out
